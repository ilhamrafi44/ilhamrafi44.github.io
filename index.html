The Dirt

Three systems, dug out and cut open. The problem, the decision, the part that was hard.

ISP PLATFORM

Twenty-seven modules, eight databases, one deployable

THE PROBLEM

An ISP's subscriber lifecycle touches billing, provisioning, payments and the network hardware itself. Split that into services and every ordinary action — activate a customer, take a payment, cut them off — becomes a distributed transaction you spend the rest of your life apologising for.

THE DECISION

One deployable, 27 modules, Spring Modulith. Hexagonal and domain-driven inside each module, events between them, and 8 PostgreSQL databases with transaction management scoped per context.

THE HARD PART

Money. It is stored as integer cents and never as a float, because a rounding error in a billing engine is not a bug you find — it is one a customer finds. Invoicing, proration, discounts and VAT feed a double-entry ledger with its own journal and chart of accounts. Payment callbacks are HMAC-verified and idempotent, because a gateway will send the same callback twice and mean it.

WHAT IT DOES NOW

Overdue subscribers are suspended and brought back the moment payment lands. Provisioning speaks FreeRADIUS and MikroTik directly — PPPoE, live sessions, CoA disconnect, RADIUS accounting — so the billing decision and the router agree without a human in between.

PAYMENTS

Proving every rupiah actually moved

THE PROBLEM

A payment gateway settles through banks. Somebody has to prove that what the gateway thinks happened, what the bank says happened, and what the back office recorded are the same three things.

THE DECISION

Two-stage reconciliation: third-party settlement against the bank mutation first, then that result against back-office records. One pass would hide which side was wrong.

THE HARD PART

Discrepancies have shapes, and each shape needs a different answer. A disputed amount is not a transaction missing in the bank, and neither is a transaction missing in the back office. Detecting them is easy; telling them apart automatically is the work.

WHAT IT DOES NOW

Merchant disbursement and deduction — fees, chargebacks, adjustments — run on the same reconciled ledger, so payouts cannot drift from what was settled.

TICKETING

Built for a queue, not for a demo

THE PROBLEM

A park gate is not a web form. The queue does not wait, the device is handheld, the signal is whatever the gate has that day, and a forged ticket has to be refused while the person holding it is standing there.

THE DECISION

Three pieces that had to agree: the public ticketing site, the back office, and a React Native gate-checker app built with Expo for the staff on the gate.

THE HARD PART

Everything at the gate happens in front of an audience. QR and barcode scanning, thermal printing, and forged-ticket rejection all had to be fast enough that the queue keeps moving and certain enough that nobody is waved through on a guess.

WHAT IT DOES NOW

Domestic ticketing for the park runs on it.
